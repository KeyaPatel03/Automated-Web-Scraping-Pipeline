Complete the FAFSA

The most important step in financing your education is filling out the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA).

You can apply online at 
https://studentaid.gov/h/apply-for-aid/fafsa
. You must complete the FAFSA to be eligible for loans, scholarships, and grants issued through the federal government and WV state programs.

What Is the FAFSA?

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid, commonly known as the FAFSA, is the standard form used to determine a student’s eligibility for federal, state, and institutional financial aid. For students attending the University of Charleston in West Virginia, the FAFSA helps assess financial need and identifies which aid programs may be available based on household income, family size, and enrollment status.

Why Completing the FAFSA Matters

Completing the FAFSA is important even if you believe you may not qualify for need- based aid. Many scholarships, grants, and low interest student loans require a completed FAFSA as part of the eligibility process. In West Virginia, state funded aid programs also rely on FAFSA information, making it a critical step for maximizing available financial assistance.

What Information Is Needed to Complete the FAFSA?

When completing the FAFSA, students should be prepared to provide basic personal information, tax and income details, and financial information for themselves and, if applicable, their parents or guardians. Using accurate and up to date information helps avoid processing delays and ensures the financial aid offer reflects your current situation.

FAFSA and Planning for College Costs

Submitting the FAFSA early allows students to better understand their potential financial aid options and plan for college related expenses such as tuition, fees, housing, and books. Reviewing your FAFSA results alongside program costs can help you make informed decisions about enrollment, budgeting, and degree planning at the University of Charleston.

          When you fill out the FAFSA application, you will need the following information:        

Previous Federal Income Tax Returns and other tax information, including W-2 statements for the:

Student

Student’s spouse (if married)

Student’s parents (if the student is dependent)

Student’s Social Security Number (and parents’ Social Security Numbers if the student is a dependent)

Driver’s license number

Alien Registration Number (if the student is a permanent resident)

Current financial information for the student and the parents (if the student is dependent) for:

Savings

Investments

Business and farm assets
